MULTI-COMPANY WORKSPACE

Company Financials & Peer Comparison

Private local records

Build a multi-year financial history for each company. Compare aligned fiscal periods, inspect peer dispersion, and chart trends for any supported metric.

Analyst checks before comparing
  • Use the same currency and scale across companies; no FX or unit conversion is performed.
  • Align fiscal year ends, reporting periods, and trailing versus annual data before interpreting peers.
  • Check accounting policies, one-off items, leases, acquisitions, and restatements for comparability.
  • A higher ratio is not always better. Interpret valuation, leverage, liquidity, and cash-cycle measures in context.
How to build a reliable peer set
  1. Add each company once and set its reporting currency and scale.
  2. For every company, save a consistent financial data set for each fiscal year.
  3. Choose the same period label, metric family, and trend metric to compare peers.

TIME VALUE OF MONEY

Future Value, Present Value & Annuities

Calculate FV, PV, or the periodic contribution for a level-payment annuity. Payments occur at the end or beginning of each period; the annual nominal rate is converted using the selected compounding frequency.

Calculated result—

Enter assumptions and calculate to see the result.

Formula & assumptions

PV and PMT are rearranged from the FV equation; N and I/Y are solved numerically.

Periodic rate i = nominal annual rate / m; number of periods n = years × m. FV = PV(1+i)^n + PMT × [((1+i)^n − 1)/i], adjusted by (1+i) for annuity due. No taxes, fees, inflation, or changing rates are modeled.

EXPECTED RETURN FRAMEWORKS

CAPM & Arbitrage Pricing Theory (APT)

Compare a single-factor CAPM estimate with a multi-factor APT estimate. Factor risk premiums are entered directly in percentage points and betas measure sensitivity to each factor.

APT factor exposures and premia

CAPM expected return—Rf + βm × (Rm − Rf)
APT expected return—Rf + Σ(βk × factor premiumk)
APT − CAPM spread—Model estimate difference; investigate factor definitions and sample.

Inputs are valid; estimates use the assumptions above.

Interpretation and model risk

CAPM is a one-factor equilibrium model. APT is a linear factor model; factor selection and premia are not uniquely determined by this calculator. These expected returns are assumptions, not guaranteed outcomes. Do not treat CAPM and APT estimates as interchangeable without checking horizon, currency, factor construction, and estimation method.

QUICK REFERENCE

Advanced Financial Formula Library

Review the structure, inputs, and interpretation limits of common CFA-style models. Expand a card for the formula and analytical checks.

WACCWeighted average cost of capital

WACC = E/(D+E) × Re + D/(D+E) × Rd × (1−T)

Use market-value weights and marginal after-tax debt cost; match nominal/real basis and currency to FCFF.
FCFFFree cash flow to the firm

FCFF = EBIT × (1−T) + D&A − CapEx − ΔNWC

Discount FCFF at WACC to estimate enterprise value; subtract net debt and other claims for equity value.
ROIC & EVAEconomic value added

ROIC = NOPAT / Average invested capital
EVA = NOPAT − WACC × Invested capital

Consistent operating scope, tax treatment, and invested-capital definition are essential.
DuPont ROEThree-factor decomposition

ROE = Net margin × Asset turnover × Equity multiplier

Separate profitability, operating efficiency, and leverage to explain changes in ROE.
Gordon GrowthDividend discount model

V₀ = D₁ / (r − g), where D₁ = D₀(1+g)

Requires r > g and sustainable constant growth; value becomes highly sensitive as r approaches g.
CCCCash conversion cycle

CCC = DSO + DIO − DPO

Use average receivables, inventory, and payables with aligned sales/COGS periods; seasonality can distort point estimates.
Bond durationModified duration and convexity

ΔP/P ≈ −Dmod × Δy + ½ × Convexity × (Δy)²

Local approximation for small parallel yield changes; embedded options and curve shifts require more advanced treatment.
Sharpe ratioExcess return per unit of total risk

Sharpe = (Rp − Rf) / σp

Returns and risk-free rate must share a frequency; standard deviation treats upside and downside volatility symmetrically.
FCFEFree cash flow to equity

FCFE = Net income + D&A − CapEx − ΔNWC + net borrowing

Discount at cost of equity. Net borrowing and reinvestment assumptions must match the forecast capital structure.
Residual incomeEquity value from book value and excess returns

V₀ = B₀ + Σ [(ROEₜ − rₑ) × Bₜ₋₁] / (1 + rₑ)ᵗ

Useful when dividends or free cash flows are difficult to forecast; book value and clean-surplus assumptions matter.
Fama–French 3-factorMarket, size, and value factor model

Ri − Rf = α + βMKT·MKT + βSMB·SMB + βHML·HML + ε

Factor returns must be aligned in time and currency; exposures are estimated, not prescribed by this formula.
